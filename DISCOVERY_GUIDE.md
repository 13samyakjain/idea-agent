# Discovery Conversation Guide — IdeaAgent Hypothesis Test

**Hypothesis being tested**: Founders want an agent-run structuring/execution layer, not just a template or a Notion doc.

**Target**: 3-5 conversations, 15-20 min each, this week.

---

## Opener (no pitch, no leading)

> "I'm trying to understand how people who've had an idea actually go from 'idea' to 'doing something about it.' Not selling anything — just want to learn how you've handled that, if it's happened to you."

If they ask what you're working on, defer: "I'll tell you at the end — don't want to bias what you say."

---

## Discovery Questions

1. Walk me through the last idea you had that you actually wanted to act on. What happened in the first week?
2. What did you use to organize it — a doc, a spreadsheet, a cofounder's brain, nothing? Why that and not something else?
3. Where did it stall, if it stalled? What was the actual moment you stopped moving on it?
4. How many ideas have you had in the last year that never got past "I should do this"? What killed them?
5. If you had unlimited hours but no help, would that idea be alive today? (Tests: is it a time problem or a structure problem?)
6. Have you ever used a tool, template, or framework to force yourself to execute? What happened to it after week 2?
7. Suppose an AI agent could not just suggest a plan but actually go do parts of it — draft the outreach, build the first version, run the research — with you reviewing its work. What's the first thing you'd want to check before trusting it with something real?
8. What would it take for you to hand a task to an agent and NOT double-check it immediately?
9. Who or what currently holds you accountable to keep going on an idea? What happens when that's absent?
10. If this exact problem got solved for you tomorrow, what would be different about your week?

---

## Confirmed vs. Weak Signals

**Confirms the hypothesis** (need 2-3 of these across interviews):
- They describe a *specific* stalled idea and can name the structural gap (no roadmap, no accountability, no next step) — not just "no time."
- They've tried and abandoned a manual system (Notion, spreadsheet, doc) specifically because *keeping it updated* or *doing the work* was the failure point, not the format.
- When asked about agent execution authority (Q7-8), they engage with *what conditions* would let them trust it — not whether they'd ever trust it at all. They're negotiating trust, not rejecting the premise.

**Weak/dead signal** (need 2-3 of these to seriously reconsider):
- They say the blocker was always time, money, or co-founder — never structure or process. Agents wouldn't have changed the outcome.
- They currently use a lightweight tool/doc and it's genuinely working for them — no expressed friction, no abandoned attempts.
- On Q7-8, they flatly reject any agent having execution authority regardless of conditions ("I'd never let AI do real work unsupervised") — not a scoping objection, a category rejection.
- They can't recall a specific stalled idea at all — the pain isn't real or isn't top-of-mind enough to act on.
