# Discovery Conversation Guide — IdeaAgent Hypothesis Test

**Hypothesis being tested**: Founders want an agent-run structuring/execution layer, not just a template or a Notion doc.

**Target**: 3-5 conversations, 15-20 min each, this week.

> Revised 2026-08-10 after review by Product Manager and Discovery Coach agents (see
> STATUS_LOG.md). Original version couldn't disconfirm its own hypothesis — no question
> directly compared "agent execution" against "just better structure," and Q7 primed an
> enthusiastic answer instead of measuring a neutral reaction. Fixed below.

---

## Opener (no pitch, no leading)

> "I'm trying to understand how people who've had an idea actually go from 'idea' to 'doing something about it.' Not selling anything — just want to learn how you've handled that, if it's happened to you."

If they ask what you're working on, defer: "I'll tell you at the end — don't want to bias what you say."

---

## Core questions (ask all of these — 15-20 min budget assumes these six)

1. **Walk me through the last idea you had that you actually wanted to act on. What happened in the first week?**
   *Nudge only if they stall, don't script it as a second question:* "What did you use to organize it, if anything?"

2. **Where did it stall, if it stalled — and why do you think it actually stalled?**
   (Root cause, asked directly — don't let them stop at "I got busy.")

3. **What do you think that stall actually cost you — time, money, or a specific opportunity you can point to?**
   (Quantifies the pain. If they can't name a cost, that itself is a data point — the pain may not be real.)

4. **If I built you a genuinely great structured template — built-in accountability nudges, reminders, milestones, no AI actually doing any of the work — would that have kept you moving?**
   (The direct comparison the hypothesis lives or dies on. A confident "yes, structure alone would've been enough" disconfirms the hypothesis regardless of how they answer Q6.)

5. **How would you feel about an AI agent actually doing parts of the work — not just planning it, but drafting outreach, building a first version, running research — while you review it?**
   (Neutral open reaction first. Do NOT lead with "what would you check before trusting it" — that presupposes they're already inclined to accept the premise.)
   *Only if they respond with curiosity or interest, not hesitation/rejection, follow with:* "What's the first thing you'd want to check before trusting it?"
   *If they respond with hesitation:* "Would you ever hand a task off like that? If so, under what conditions?" — leave room for a clean no.

6. **If this exact problem got solved for you tomorrow, what would be different about your week?**

---

## Optional follow-ups (use if time and conversation allow — don't force all four)

7. **Why is this on your mind now, rather than six months ago or six months from now?** (urgency/trigger check)
8. **Have you ever used a tool, template, or an AI tool like ChatGPT/Claude to force yourself to execute on something like this? What happened to it after week 2?** (revealed preference — has this person already tried and abandoned something)
9. **Have you already tried using an AI agent for something like this specifically?** What happened? (revealed preference beats hypothetical willingness — weight this above Q5 if they have a real answer)
10. **Who or what currently holds you accountable to keep going on an idea? What happens when that's absent?**

---

## Confirmed vs. Weak Signals

**Confirms the hypothesis** (need 2-3 of these across interviews, and Q4 must NOT be a confident "template alone would've worked"):
- On Q4, they say structure/template alone would *not* have been enough — something needs to actually do work, not just remind them.
- They describe a *specific* stalled idea (Q1-2) and can name the structural gap themselves, unprompted — not just "no time."
- They've tried and abandoned a manual system (Q8) specifically because *keeping it updated* or *doing the work* was the failure point, not the format.
- On Q5/optional Q9, they engage with *what conditions* would let them trust agent execution — not whether they'd ever trust it at all — AND ideally have a revealed-preference data point (Q9) supporting it, not just hypothetical enthusiasm.

**Weak/dead signal** (need 2-3 of these to seriously reconsider):
- On Q4, they say a good template/checklist alone would have been enough — this directly disconfirms the "needs execution, not structure" hypothesis.
- They describe the stall as purely time, money, or co-founder — with no prompting toward structure or execution gaps — on Q2.
- They currently use a lightweight tool/doc and it's genuinely working for them (Q8) — no expressed friction, no abandoned attempts.
- On Q5, they express hesitation or rejection about agent execution, even after being asked under what conditions.
- They can't name a cost on Q3 and can't recall a specific stalled idea on Q1 — the pain isn't real or isn't top-of-mind enough to act on.

**Confirmation-bias guard**: the founder running these interviews is also the one holding the hypothesis. Log verbatim quotes (below) and score the confirm/weak-signal criteria against the quotes *after* all interviews are done, not in the moment — don't let a good rapport in the room inflate the read.

**What hitting the confirm bar actually justifies**: not a build decision. 3-5 friendly network conversations at this bar justify running 5 more interviews with a working prototype and a real commitment ask (waitlist, paid pilot, scheduled test) — revealed preference beats stated preference. Don't treat "2-3 of 3" from this round as a green light to start building.
