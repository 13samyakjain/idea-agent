# ACS Consultancy Services — Re-Qualification (Sales Engine batch-1)

**Verdict: DISQUALIFY.** Drop from batch-1.

## Company facts
NY State/City certified MWBE, IT solutions/consulting for **public sector** clients (state
agencies, local government, school districts). Latham NY, ~18 employees (per Stage-1 record;
public sources put current headcount higher), founded 2011, ~$3M revenue. 2025 Inc. 5000
honoree, 254% 3-yr growth — and this is a repeat honor (also listed in 2020 with 638% 3-yr
growth), so the growth pattern is sustained, not a one-off. Decision-maker: Asha Ramrakhiani,
Founder & President/CEO.

## Sales-motion analysis
ACS does not sell IT services door-to-door or through referrals — it competes for **government
contract vehicles** and the task orders issued under them:
- Holds an **NY OGS** statewide IT consulting RFP award (RFP 23269), a **Texas DIR ITSAC**
  contract, an **Oklahoma (SW1025) IT staff-aug** contract, and a **GSA Schedule**
  (47QTCA22D001Y) — i.e., pre-qualified vendor status across multiple government buying
  channels, not one relationship.
- Runs a dedicated `RFP@acsincny.com` intake, implying a standing proposal/bid-response
  function, not ad hoc founder outreach.
- MWBE certification is itself a growth lever: it unlocks agency set-aside spend and
  diversity-procurement quotas that a non-certified competitor structurally cannot access.

This is a **procurement/certification-driven motion**: win a contract vehicle once, then
compete for/receive task orders under it, repeatable across agencies and states. It scales by
adding contract vehicles and certifications, not by an individual rep's outbound capacity — the
opposite of the recruiting/staffing agencies in this batch (Hire Resolve, Egger & Co, Pocketbook,
Smith Family), whose growth genuinely is capped by founder network and referral flow.

## Why this fails the Sales Engine's target problem
Glimpse's Sales Engine is built for companies that "don't know how to sell what they already
sell / can't scale their existing sales motion" — i.e., outbound/relationship sales capacity is
the bottleneck. ACS's growth ceiling, if any, is **contract-vehicle acquisition and RFP
win-rate/response capacity** — a compliance-and-bid-writing problem, not an outbound-prospecting
problem. Even 254% growth with only ~18 employees suggests the constraint is delivery/bid
bandwidth, not lead generation: they aren't short on demand signal (RFPs are public and
scheduled), they're competing on proposal quality and past-performance scoring. Glimpse's
signal-based outbound/multi-channel sequence playbook has no lever here — there's no persona to
cold-email into a discovery call when the buying process is a formal, published solicitation.
The Stage-1 checklist passed ACS on generic firmographics (revenue, no BD hire, operating
history, named decision-maker) but never tested motion-fit, which is exactly the gap this
re-qualification was asked to close.

## Outcome
Remove ACS from batch-1 first-touch outreach. No replacement outreach line is needed since no
outreach should be sent — the existing "immigration/consultancy" draft line was wrong (ACS is
IT/public-sector consulting, not immigration) but that correction is moot under a disqualify;
flagging the factual error here so it isn't reused if ACS is ever reconsidered under a different
offer (e.g., a future "public-sector proposal support" product, not Sales Engine).
